Theatre & music

MÜPA Syrius — Staged concert

In Shattered Dreams (Széttört álmok), the now-classic music of the legendary band Syrius is reimagined in 21st-century arrangements by the Hungarian Frank Zappa Memorial Band, joined by outstanding musicians including Ádám Török, Béla Szakcsi Lakatos, Tibor Tátrai and former Syrius keyboardist László Pataki. Horváth Charlie also appeared as a surprise guest. The production premiered at the Palace of Arts (Müpa Budapest) on 25 May 2012. Dance by the Szeged Contemporary Dance Company and Aximmetry’s visuals open up new dimensions in translating music into images.

The production brings Syrius’s compositions to the stage in a form that combines a traditional concert, a visual theatre show and a contemporary dance performance. A distinctive feature is Aximmetry’s real-time projection, which gives the musicians and dancers freedom in their performance and choreography, unlike conventional video projections tied to a fixed timeline.

Alongside the band, the artist controlling the projections is an integral part of the onstage ensemble. A large, transparent, futuristic touchscreen makes this creative process visible to the audience, offering a glimpse behind the scenes.

Spaces & occasions

Events & exhibitions

At exhibition openings for Tamás Konok, a Kossuth Prize laureate, internationally recognised Constructivist painter and leading figure in Hungarian contemporary art, slowly evolving geometric projections complemented his modern paintings. Visual elements appeared in rhythmic harmony with transcendent background music, echoing the character of his work.

At weddings and corporate events, a diverse visual palette created a distinctive atmosphere, with imagery projected directly onto the dance floor and performing artists.

For business events, the work typically involved panoramic projection using multiple projectors, video splitting and media server operation, ensuring seamless presentation of other artists’ work.

Archive / 2005–2012

Party projections — The early years

Party visuals remain an integral part of the underground music scene and have evolved enormously over the years. Early setups relied on analogue equipment and video synthesizers; as PCs became more powerful, new approaches made greater interactivity possible. AVImpulse was one of the collectives experimenting with these possibilities. Reactive, minimalist Flash animations and a custom-built, MIDI-enabled DJ audio mixer defined this period, with sharp visual cuts keeping the projections in sync with the rhythm of the music.

Another distinctive experiment involved projecting dense, textural visuals directly onto the audience. The crowd became an organic surface, bringing the imagery to life together with the music and the movement of the participants.

Archive / 2012–1018

Aximmetry — Generative visuals

A collaborative development effort led to Performer, a dedicated branch of Aximmetry’s node-based visual synthesizer and 3D engine, designed for expressive, virtuoso improvisation through touchscreen and gesture control. A large, semi-transparent “holographic” control panel added a striking visual dimension: positioned on stage facing the audience, the performer appeared to be operating a science-fiction console brought to life.

Its significance went beyond the spectacular interface. It offered a compelling alternative to the complex, cumbersome node-based systems that dominated the field at the time. Elaborate multi-projector setups, projection mapping and seamlessly aligned panoramic projections combined with never-repeating live generative visuals to create a grand spectacle, all running efficiently on a single computer.
